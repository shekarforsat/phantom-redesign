Phantom, redesigned: know the true cost before you sign.

A redesign of two Phantom interactions that quietly cost users money: the swap flow, where price impact and slippage hide in fine print, and the activity feed, where your own money history is written in raw transaction hashes. The screens below are the deliverable — before/after, mobile and desktop — followed by an interactive prototype of the redesigned swap and the full write-up.

Principle 1 — State the cost before the signature, not after. Principle 2 — Verified by default; lookalikes never look identical. Principle 3 — Activity speaks human, details on demand.
Feature 1 of 2 · Swap

The swap that tells you what it costs

In today's Phantom, a swap review shows two numbers and a button. Everything that determines whether the trade is good or bad — price impact, minimum received, route quality, whether the token is even real — is either invisible or set in 10px grey type. Beginners sign blind; experienced users leave the app to check.

PROBLEM 1

Price impact is nowhere

The review screen never shows price impact. On a thin pool, a user can lose several percent to impact and only discover it by comparing balances afterwards.

PROBLEM 2

Slippage is fine print

Slippage tolerance — the setting that decides how bad a fill you will accept — sits in tiny grey text under the button. “Minimum received” is never stated at all.

PROBLEM 3

Fake tokens look real

In the token picker, a scam token with a copied name and symbol renders identically to the genuine one. The only differentiator is a truncated mint address in 9px type.

Desktop uses its width: the true-cost panel becomes persistent, and routes are compared side by side instead of hiding behind a single “best price” claim.

Decision — cost is a card, not a footnote

Impact, slippage, route and fee live in one “True cost” card with a single total. If a number matters at signing time, it is set at reading size, in words a first-time user understands (“guaranteed or the swap cancels”).

Decision — slippage you can touch

The tolerance is a segmented control on the review screen itself, and minimum-received updates with it. Users learn what slippage does by changing it, not by reading a tooltip.

Decision — verification is the default sort

The picker opens on verified tokens. Lookalikes are collapsed behind a labelled warning row — visible enough to be honest, demoted enough to be safe.

Decision — nothing is removed

Every expert affordance survives: custom slippage, unverified tokens, full routes. The redesign changes defaults and emphasis, not capabilities.

Feature 2 of 2 · Activity

Activity that reads like a bank statement, not a block explorer

Phantom’s activity feed is a list of arrows and truncated hashes. To answer the simplest question — “what did I actually do yesterday, and did it work?” — users copy a signature into an explorer. A failed swap looks identical to a successful one, so people retry transactions that already failed for a reason they never see.

PROBLEM 1

Hashes are the headline

Each row leads with an arrow and a truncated signature. The human facts — what was swapped, for what, at what fee — are absent.

PROBLEM 2

Failure is invisible

A swap that failed on slippage renders exactly like a confirmed one. No reason, no reassurance that no funds were lost, no next step.

PROBLEM 3

No grouping, no story

A swap’s approve/route/settle steps and a morning’s related actions scatter as isolated rows. The feed has data, but no narrative.

Desktop earns a detail pane: selecting a row shows the plain-language summary, the economics, and the full on-chain reference — the explorer is one click away, not the price of understanding.

Decision — sentences, not signatures

Every row is a sentence a person would say: “Swapped 2.5 SOL → 453.42 USDC”. Counterparty, venue and fee ride along as a quiet subline. The signature still exists — one tap deeper.

Decision — failure is a first-class state

Failed transactions get an explanation card in the feed itself: what happened, that no funds were taken, and the single sensible next action. Support tickets and blind retries both start here.

Decision — group by day and session

Rows group under Today / Yesterday, and related actions cluster. Scanning a week of activity becomes reading a short diary, not paging a database.

Decision — status is always visible

Confirmed / Failed is a chip on every row, never a detail to excavate. Colour is backed by text, so the state survives colour-blindness and screenshots.

Why it worksThe teaching moment

Most users have never changed a slippage setting, because nothing in the current UI connects the setting to a consequence. Here the connection is mechanical and immediate:

  • 0.1% — minimum received rises; the note warns the swap may fail if the price moves.
  • 0.5% — the balanced default for liquid pairs.
  • 1–3% — minimum received drops visibly, and the note names the risk (worse fills, MEV) instead of silently accepting it.

Editing the SOL amount rescales the whole card, including price impact — size is what creates impact, and the interface finally says so.

Design system

Evolved, not replaced

The redesign keeps Phantom’s identity — near-black surfaces, the lavender accent, the ghost’s rounded geometry — and adds what the current system lacks: semantic colour for money states and a type scale that gives cost information reading size. Nothing decorative was added; every new element carries information.

Type

Amounts at 26px/750 are the loudest element on any money screen. Labels drop to 11px uppercase grey. Explanations sit at 12–13px secondary — readable, never competing with the number they explain.

Radius & spacing

One radius scale (8 / 12 / 16) inside the product, 4px spacing grid throughout. Cards separate by surface shift, not borders — the single amber edge on the cost card is the only loud border, and it means “this costs you”.

Colour discipline

Amber is reserved for cost and caution, green for confirmed and received, red for loss and failure. Colour always travels with text, so meaning survives greyscale and colour-blindness.

Write-up

The problems, and what I changed

The app I redesigned: Phantom — the most widely used Solana wallet, and the first crypto product most newcomers ever touch. I redesigned two flows: Swap (review + token picker) and Activity, on mobile and desktop.

Problem 1 — a swap’s real cost is invisible at the moment of decision. Phantom’s review screen shows the amounts and a rate. Price impact — often the largest cost of a trade — appears nowhere. Slippage tolerance, the setting that defines the worst fill a user accepts, is rendered in 10px grey type beneath the call to action, and “minimum received” is never stated. The predictable result: users sign swaps they would have resized or abandoned, and learn about the cost from their balance.

What I changed: a single True Cost card on the review screen — price impact in dollars and percent with a one-line explanation, slippage as a tappable segmented control, the winning route named, the network fee, and one total. “Minimum received” is promoted into the receive card as a guarantee (“guaranteed or the swap cancels”). On desktop the card becomes a persistent side panel and the routes checked are compared openly, so “best price” is a claim the user can verify.

Problem 2 — the token picker can’t tell real from fake. A lookalike token with a copied name and symbol renders identically to the genuine asset; the only tell is a truncated mint address in small type. This is a safety failure presented as a list.

What I changed: the picker defaults to registry-verified tokens with a visible verified state and issuer context (“by Circle · deep liquidity”). Unverified lookalikes collapse into a single labelled warning row — never silently deleted, because experts legitimately trade unverified tokens, but never again visually identical to the real thing.

Problem 3 — activity is written for explorers, not people. Rows lead with truncated signatures; there is no status, no counterparty story, no fee. A failed swap is indistinguishable from a confirmed one, which produces blind retries and support tickets.

What I changed: every row becomes a sentence (“Swapped 2.5 SOL → 453.42 USDC · via Orca · fee ≈ $0.01”) with a status chip; rows group by day; failures get an explanation card in the feed itself — what happened, that no funds were taken, and the next action. Signatures move to a detail view (a side pane on desktop) with a one-click path to the explorer.

Principles behind the decisions:

  • State the cost before the signature. Anything that changes what a user would decide belongs before the button, at reading size.
  • Safe defaults, open doors. Verified-first and balanced slippage protect newcomers; every expert path (custom slippage, unverified tokens, raw signatures) remains one tap away.
  • Explain at the point of action. Each number that could confuse gets one plain sentence, where it appears — not in a help centre.

Honest limits: this is an unvalidated design concept. Figures are illustrative, screens are a faithful reconstruction of current Phantom patterns rather than traced screenshots, and the next step would be moderated usability tests of the review screen with first-time swappers — especially whether the True Cost card changes trade size or abandonment. I’d rather ship that test than claim results I don’t have.
